# HR Analytics Insights

## Overview

This analysis was conducted on a 300-employee HR dataset to understand workforce distribution, attrition patterns, and the relationship between compensation and employee retention.

---

## Key Findings

* Total employees: **300**

* Workforce is fairly distributed across departments, with Operations (61) and Marketing (58) having the largest headcounts, while HR has the smallest (30).

* **Attrition rates vary significantly by department:**

  * Sales: **25.45%** (highest)
  * IT: **24.00%**
  * HR: **20.00%**
  * Operations: **19.67%**
  * Marketing: **12.07%**
  * Finance: **10.87%** (lowest)

* **Sales and IT show the highest attrition risk**, indicating potential issues related to workload pressure, role expectations, or career progression.

* Compensation does not fully explain attrition behavior:

  * Finance has the **highest average salary (~117K)** and the **lowest attrition**, indicating strong retention stability.
  * IT has one of the **highest average salaries (~112K)** but still experiences high attrition, suggesting non-salary factors are influencing employee turnover.
  * Sales shows **high attrition despite mid-level salary levels**, possibly due to performance pressure or incentive structure.

* Employees with shorter tenure (early-career employees) are likely contributing disproportionately to attrition trends (based on observed patterns in dataset structure).

---

## Business Interpretation

* High attrition in **Sales and IT** signals potential retention challenges in critical revenue and technical teams.
* Salary alone is not a sufficient retention driver, especially in technical roles.
* Finance appears to have a stable workforce structure combining high compensation with low turnover.

---

## Recommendations

* Investigate **Sales and IT departments** for potential workload imbalance, performance pressure, or career progression gaps.
* Strengthen **early-career retention programs**, especially for employees within their first 1–2 years.
* Review non-salary retention factors such as:

  * Career development opportunities
  * Work-life balance
  * Role expectations clarity
* Maintain Finance’s current structure while using it as a benchmark for retention best practices.

---

## Conclusion

This analysis demonstrates how SQL can be used to transform raw HR data into actionable business insights that support workforce planning and retention strategy decisions.
