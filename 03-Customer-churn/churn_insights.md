# Customer Churn Analysis Insights

---

## 1. Dataset Overview

This analysis is based on **500 telecom customers** with subscription, behavioral, and financial attributes.

* Total Customers: **500**
* Churned Customers: **172**
* Retained Customers: **328**
* Overall Churn Rate: **34.4%**

This indicates that more than **1 in 3 customers are leaving the service**, representing a significant retention challenge.

---

## 2. Executive Summary

Customer churn is primarily driven by **contract structure, early customer lifecycle experience, and service engagement levels**.

The analysis shows that pricing alone is not the dominant factor — instead, churn is strongly influenced by commitment level and perceived service value.

---

## 3. Key Churn Drivers

---

### 3.1 Contract Type (Strongest Driver)

* Month-to-month customers: **~50% churn rate**
* One-year contracts: **~22.5% churn rate**
* Two-year contracts: **~15.2% churn rate**

**Insight:** Longer contractual commitments significantly improve customer retention by reducing flexibility-driven churn.

---

### 3.2 Payment Method Behavior

* Electronic Check users show the highest churn (~48%)
* Credit Card and Bank Transfer users show significantly lower churn (~27%)

**Insight:** Customers using automated payment methods are more stable, suggesting that convenience and engagement level reduce churn likelihood.

---

### 3.3 Customer Tenure (Lifecycle Risk)

* Highest churn occurs within the **first 12 months**
* Customers beyond **24 months show significantly lower churn**

**Insight:** The first year is the most critical retention period, where onboarding experience determines long-term loyalty.

---

### 3.4 Monthly Charges and Pricing Impact

* High-paying customers (> $70/month) show elevated churn risk
* Medium-priced customers contribute the highest churn volume

**Insight:** Churn is not purely price-driven; it is strongly linked to perceived value relative to cost.

---

### 3.5 Service Engagement (Tech Support Impact)

* Customers without Tech Support churn at significantly higher rates
* Online Backup and support services improve retention behavior

**Insight:** Service adoption increases stickiness and reduces churn likelihood.

---

## 4. Revenue at Risk

A significant portion of revenue risk is concentrated among:

* Month-to-month customers
* Early-stage customers (0–12 months tenure)
* High-charge, low-engagement users

**Insight:** Targeted intervention in these segments can significantly reduce overall churn impact.

---

## 5. Strategic Recommendations

---

### 5.1 Contract Optimization Strategy

Encourage migration from month-to-month plans to longer-term contracts through:

* Discounts
* Bundled services
* Loyalty incentives

---

### 5.2 Early Lifecycle Retention Program

Focus heavily on the first 12 months through:

* Onboarding journeys
* Customer check-ins
* Early engagement incentives

---

### 5.3 Payment Method Migration

Encourage customers to switch from Electronic Check to:

* Credit Card
* Bank Transfer (auto-pay systems)

---

### 5.4 Service Bundling Strategy

Bundle Tech Support and Online Backup into higher-tier plans to:

* Increase retention
* Improve perceived value
* Reduce churn in high-risk segments

---

### 5.5 Churn Risk Scoring System

Develop a simple risk scoring model using:

* Contract type
* Tenure
* Monthly charges
* Service adoption

This can enable proactive identification of high-risk customers before churn occurs.

---

## 6. Final Conclusion

Customer churn in this dataset is primarily driven by **engagement, commitment level, and early customer experience rather than pricing alone**.

Reducing churn will require a combination of:

* stronger contract structures
* improved onboarding experience
* increased service adoption
* proactive risk identification

Overall, the business has clear opportunities to significantly improve retention through targeted, data-driven interventions.
